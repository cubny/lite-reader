Understanding Modern Web Development

The full article body covers progressive web apps, server-side rendering, and the trade-offs of API-first design in production systems serving millions of requests every day. The discussion goes far beyond the short summary that ships in the RSS feed teaser.

Server-side rendering is back, and for good reason: hydration costs have become measurable, search engines reward fast time-to-first-byte, and the operational story for SSR has matured considerably. Frameworks like Next, Remix, and SvelteKit converge on similar primitives.

Edge runtimes change the latency calculus for read-heavy workloads. When you can run handlers within fifty milliseconds of the user, you stop paying for round trips to a single origin region. That is the real unlock, not the marketing copy about "global functions".

None of this matters without operational maturity. Observability, load shedding, structured logging, and replay-friendly idempotency keys remain the unsexy work that determines whether a system survives its first real traffic spike.
